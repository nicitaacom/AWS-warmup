Relative Adaptation

Relative adaptation is the single most important thing you need to understand to write winning cold emails.

It basically means understanding your prospect, and moulding your cold emails accordingly.

Never write what you want to write. Write what they want to read.

Base every cold email writing decision on your prospect and what they want/need, not what you want/need. Relative adaptation means to adapt your body copy, subject line & CTA to your prospect.

Think of it like nature: If nature can produce the human race over 6,000,000 years (the most adapted species to earth ever) then we can use its natural selection playbook to produce a half-decent cold email.

Your cold email is an organism. Your niche/market is an environment.

For the organism to survive it must be well adapted to the environment. 

The environment is ever changing, and your cold emails must change accordingly. Ever wondered why some copy that worked a year ago doesn’t work now? Change.

“It is not the strongest nor the fittest to survive, but those most adapted to the environment in which they find themselves in” - Darwin Summarised 

But how do we achieve relative adaptation?

Well, to adapt to our prospects we have to understand them.

Click-Whirr Response Algorithm

All humans are creatures of conditioning.

Conditioning is the word used to explain the relationship between stimulus and response.

When exposed to a specific stimulus, a human will respond with a specific behaviour.

Examples:

A man with a nicotine addiction: if exposed to a packet of cigarettes, then he smokes.
A proud father with a son: if his son scores a goal at a football match, then he cheers.
Person with strong left wing bias: if seeing a right-wing tweet, then they get angry.
Person with strong right wing bias: if seeing a left-wing tweet, then they get angry.
Degenerates: if exposed to an attractive woman on IG, then they visit a specific website.
You: if I make a slightly off-base political joke, then you laugh.
Me: if no one laughs at my jokes, then I cry.
You on YouTube: if exposed to the thumbnail and title, then you click.

Click, Whirr… it’s like a machine that executes on a task without thinking.
Click-Whirr responses are “if this, then that” statements.
They are behavioural cause and effect.

Because of this algorithm, there are specific stimuli that you can put in your cold email to elicit the response of reply/booking.

Example: ‘pay on results’ for business owners.

Example: if I understand the core pain points of an agency owner and put them in my cold email, I may well get a reply by the Click,Whirr Response…

The reason you need to understand this is simple. Humans don’t really think when clearing out to, replying to or dealing with their email inbox. 90% of whether they respond, what they respond and everything they do in their inbox is conditioned. 

The human mind craves efficiency. Because of this it has a tendency to ‘box’ stimuli together to deal with them easier. (see examples on the next page)

What this means is that if, at first glance, your email LOOKS very similar as a general stimulus to all the other emails, the prospect won’t even read it. They’ll disregard it.














My unconscious brain has seen these stimuli hundreds of times, I read the first 5-10 probably, wasn’t convinced and eventually just started boxing them into the same category: useless, delete.

Because of this we need to introduce ‘pattern interrupts’.

Pattern interrupts are simply things you can put in your emails that break the pattern. When you do this, the prospect cannot box the email into the stimuli box they always use, and it forces them to think.

Productivity Principle

You have to understand: business owners and normal people treat their inboxes as to-do lists. If the email can’t be ‘done’ (doesn’t have a simple, definitive CTA) they will simply discard it. This is because when people are doing their email, they don’t want to think.

When someone is going through their email, all they are doing is organising chaos into order. 

If your email can’t be read and acted on within 8.25 seconds, they aren’t gonna do anything.

The exception to this is if you’re using a loom video, but if they can’t access the loom video within 8.25 seconds, you’re done for.

Keep sentences short, use simple words & stay focused on what matters.


Feature Circumvention

Focus on the outcome.

When drafting your cold email, don’t discuss the features of your products. Discuss the benefits. If you sell facebook ads, talk about clients and appointments, not facebook ads.

As soon as you mention features, you’re doomed.

Ego = Waterloo

Do not talk about yourself or your company. Seriously. Don’t.

They do not care. They care what you can do for them, not your awards, team size, location, offices, or anything. They just don’t care. You might, they don’t.

The only exception is if it’s highly relevant. 

For example in the early days of Imperium (when selling to agency owners) we included this in our cold email:

“We actually scaled our own agency to 7 figures, signing over 350+ clients and are now helping others do the same”.




Buzzword Omission

People do not read emails. They scan them, so you need to write accordingly.

When scanning, their brain is looking for buzzwords that will indicate the email is deletable. 

This is a component of the click, whirr… response. 

Examples of some buzzwords to avoid:

Guarantee
Facebook Ads
Marketing Agency
Website
Connect
SEO
Blog
Tools
Revenue
Free
Audit
30 days
Industry

The human brain is a pattern recognition machine. If your email has buzzwords that all the other emails have, they will be scanned and your email will be categorised by the algorithm as ‘delete’.

If you want to get to grips with what you really need to avoid, register a fake email in your niche and make it accessible to the cold emailers. Read them all and start to observe the patterns.


A Friend

People respond to friends, so your emails need to look as if they were sent from a friend.

Would you, if messaging a friend, use a complex footer? No.
Would you, if messaging a friend, say ‘kind regards’? No.
Would you, if messaging a friend, try to be super professional? No.
Would you, if messaging a friend, aim to have 110% perfect grammar? No.
Would you, if messaging a friend, write like you have a rod stuck up your ass? No.

Warning: you don’t want to take this to the extreme of treating people like your friends, but it pays to remember you’re contacting a human being. Not your boss. Relax.


Hu + Cr = A

Humour + Creativity = Appointments.

Make them laugh, or at least smile.

Demonstrate at least some level of creativity for god's sake.

You’ll get appointments.


Ta + Rr = A

Tangibility + Risk Reversal = Appointments.

When making an offer, make the outcome tangible. They need to be able to visualise it.

Then, reverse the risk. To reply, they need to feel reassured you’re not gonna ruin their life.

You’ll get appointments.


In + Cu = A

Incentive + Curiosity = Appointments.

Give them an incentive to reply, say you can give them what they want, what they need.

Don’t tell them everything. Leave them guessing, be vague. Invite questions, create mystery.

You’ll get appointments.


Addition From Subtraction

Less is more. Use less words where possible, say as little as you can. Try to remove as much as possible without making the email completely bare.

Humans don’t tackle paragraphs when reading/scanning cold emails, so don’t write them. 
Humans don’t read stories when reading/scanning cold emails, so don’t write them.
Humans optimise for efficiency when in to-do mode, so write your emails accordingly.

The less words you use, the more power those words have. So if you use a few words, but the right ones, they have true power.

LESS. IS. MORE.

The Footer Paradox

With your footer:

Do not include your website
Do not include your linkedin
Do not include your facebook page
Do not include your awards
Do not include your blog
Do not include your free ebook

A complex footer is simply someone documenting their stupidity. 

Simple, clean and undistracted. More importantly, slightly differently to everyone else.

This was the footer I used to get 10,000 appointments via cold email.

Footers should build trust but not add any call to actions or ruin the prospects focus.




Single Path

Your email should be focused on one goal: to get an appointment.

So focus on that. Ask them to do nothing but book a call.

You need to give your prospects a single path. 

No facebook group
No newsletter registration
No ebook
No free audit
No website link

Nothing. Just the call. Maybe a loom (this is the only real exception).






Open to?

When making your call to action, ask if they’re open to the idea of the action instead of demanding it straight up.

E.g. 

Instead of:

“Would you be interested in booking a call this week to discuss?”


We’d use:

“Would you be open to the idea of booking a call this week to discuss?”

It’s easier to agree to the idea of something than to the thing itself. Just a tip!


Aftercare

Understand: 80% of your meetings will come in the aftercare.

What is aftercare? Followup.

Follow up on every prospect at least 5-6 times.

Most of your replies will come on follow up 4,5 & 6.

It might sound like overkill, but do it. Every 2 days, send a simple bump followup.

Thank me later!


Pareto’s Outcome Focus

80% of your email copy needs to be focused on the outcome you can help your prospect achieve, or on reinforcing the fact you can achieve the outcome (conviction).

Outcome, future state, desires, needs, wants. This is the main thing.

If you have added something to your email that doesn’t explain the outcome or add conviction as to why you can achieve it, you’re adding the wrong thing.
